Experience Homemade Pasta Excellence with Every Bite

Flour, eggs, patience. A practical guide to making pasta at home.

This article is available in English only.

Making pasta at home turns a simple meal into something you remember. The texture, the flavour and the process itself bring a kind of satisfaction that a packet never will. Beginner or not, learning to make pasta from scratch opens up a lot of room to play.

Why bother making it yourself

Freshness comes first. Pasta made from scratch is flour, eggs and water — no preservatives, no additives. The result is tender and silky, it cooks in a couple of minutes and it takes sauce far better.

Then there is control. You choose the thickness, the shape and the dough. Spinach or beetroot puree for colour, semolina for bite, a softer dough for filled shapes.

And finally, it connects you to a craft that is centuries old. That counts for something.

What you actually need

  • Plain flour, or "00" flour for a finer texture
  • Fresh eggs for richness and structure
  • A pinch of salt
  • A little water or olive oil to adjust the dough

For tools: a clean surface, a rolling pin or pasta machine, a sharp knife or cutter, and a rack to rest the pasta on. A machine helps with consistency, but hands work fine.

The dough, step by step

  • Mix the flour and salt on a clean surface and make a well in the centre.
  • Crack the eggs into the well and beat them with a fork, drawing in flour from the edges.
  • Knead for 8–10 minutes until smooth and elastic. Add a splash of water if it feels dry.
  • Rest the dough, wrapped, for at least 30 minutes. This relaxes the gluten and makes rolling far easier.
  • Roll it out to your desired thickness, evenly.
  • Cut into fettuccine, tagliatelle or ravioli.
  • Cook immediately in boiling salted water for 1–3 minutes.

Five things that make the difference

  • Use "00" flour if you can find it — the dough is noticeably silkier.
  • Rest the dough properly or it will shrink back as you roll.
  • Roll evenly so it cooks evenly.
  • Watch the pot. Fresh pasta is done long before dried pasta would be.
  • Always save a cup of pasta water. It is the best sauce adjuster there is.

Shapes and fillings

  • Fettuccine or tagliatelle — the classic long ribbons.
  • Ravioli — ricotta and spinach, or mushroom.
  • Tortellini — meat or cheese blends.
  • Orecchiette — little ears, perfect for chunky sauces.

Serving and storing

Fresh pasta cooks in 1 to 3 minutes. Drain it and toss it straight into the sauce. Simple tomato with garlic and basil, pesto, or brown butter and sage all work beautifully.

To keep it: dry it on a rack for a few hours until firm, then store in an airtight container in the fridge for up to two days. For longer, freeze it flat on a tray, then bag it and cook it straight from frozen with an extra minute.

The best part

Making pasta is not only about food. Rolling dough, cutting shapes and filling ravioli with other people around the table is the point. Come and eat with us, or make a batch at home — either way, it is worth the flour on the bench.
